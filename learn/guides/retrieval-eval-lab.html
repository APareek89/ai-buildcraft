Step 1

Where retrieval eval sits

Retrieval is measured before the LLM writes anything. If the right evidence never arrives, no prompt can save the answer. So you score retrieval on its own.

Run this for every question in the golden set (say 200 questions). Compute metrics per question, then take the average. That average is your retrieval score.

Step 2

What a golden set row looks like

A golden set is a list of real questions with the known correct answer and the known source. Build it from real user queries, SME input, and production failures. Start with 50–200 rows.

Step 3

How do you decide a chunk is "relevant"?

Every metric depends on this yes/no label. There are three ways to get it.

A · ID match

Is the retrieved chunk ID in the gold ID list?

exact, free breaks when you re-chunk

Good for a stable corpus and CI tests.

B · Cosine vs gold chunk

Embed retrieved chunk and gold chunk. If similarity > threshold, call it relevant.

threshold is arbitrary measures topic, not evidence

Weakest option. See section 6.

C · LLM judge

Ask an LLM: "Does this chunk contain info needed to answer this question?" → yes/no.

survives re-chunking costs money, needs checking

What RAGAS does. Validate the judge on ~50 human-labelled rows first.

Common practice: store gold answer + gold source document IDs. Use the LLM judge at chunk level. Use ID match in CI where speed matters.

Step 4

Metrics lab

One question. The retriever returned 10 chunks in rank order. Click a chunk to mark it relevant or not. Change k (how many you pass to the LLM) and R (how many relevant chunks exist in the whole corpus). All numbers update with the working shown.

Your question answered: 10 should come, 5 came

Yes. If R = 10 relevant chunks exist and 5 of them are in your top-k, Recall@k = 5 / 10 = 0.5. Recall always divides by what should have come. Precision divides by what did come (k). Try it: set R = 10 above.

Catch: you must know R. With ID labels you know it. With an LLM judge you usually don't know every relevant chunk in the corpus, which is why RAGAS measures recall against the claims in the gold answer instead (next section).

Step 5

Claim-level recall (how RAGAS does it)

Split the gold answer into single facts (claims). For each claim, the judge asks: is this supported by any retrieved chunk? Recall = supported claims ÷ total claims. Tick what your retrieved chunks support.

Q: Can an Enterprise user on annual billing get a refund after upgrading?

Why better: it does not need the full list of relevant chunks, and it survives re-chunking. It asks the question that matters: is the evidence for every part of the answer present?

RAGAS context precision (rank-aware)

RAGAS precision is not plain "relevant ÷ k". It rewards relevant chunks that sit near the top, because LLMs pay more attention to early context.

Context Precision@K = Σi=1..K ( Precision@i × reli ) ÷ (number of relevant chunks in top K)

reli is 1 if chunk i is relevant, else 0. This is the "AP" card in the lab above. Same chunks, better order → higher score.

Step 6

Why cosine similarity is a weak judge

Embeddings capture topic. Relevance needs evidence. A chunk on the same topic that lacks the answer can score higher than the chunk that has it.

No threshold gets both right. That is the trap. The LLM judge reads the text and says chunk 2 has the answer and chunk 1 does not.

Step 7

Diagnose, then fix

Read recall and precision together. The pair tells you the cause.

Precision high
Precision low
Recall high
Healthy. Move on to generation eval.
Noisy. Right chunks found, buried in junk. Fix ranking.
Recall low
Too narrow. What comes is good, but pieces missing. Widen search.
Broken. Check chunking, embeddings, and whether the doc is even indexed.

Pick a symptom to see the fixes, in the order to try them.

The k trade-off. Raising k lifts recall and drops precision. Standard pattern: retrieve wide (k = 30–50) for recall, then rerank and keep narrow (k = 5–8) for precision.

Interview line: "I evaluate retrieval separately from generation. Recall is my ceiling, precision is my noise. I label with an LLM judge validated against human labels, track claim-level recall and rank-aware precision, and fix recall with hybrid search and query rewriting, precision with a reranker."
