Hidden Markov models and Viterbi

1. The one-sentence version

Hidden Markov models and Viterbi converts a particular structural assumption into a practical prediction or representation.

2. The intuition

Think of hidden markov models and viterbi as a compact rule for deciding which observations should influence one another; the rule helps only when that notion of relatedness matches the problem.

A five-item example

For [0.1,0.3,2.7,-2.2,0.2], emissions favour volatility twice; Viterbi also charges for entering and leaving, encouraging one coherent spell.

3. The maths in plain English

\[\delta_t(s)=\max_r[\delta_{t-1}(r)+\log a_{rs}]+\log p(x_t\mid s)\]

δ is the best path score ending in state s, ars the transition probability from r, and p(xt|s) the state-specific emission likelihood.

4. What the model is actually doing during training

  1. Initialize state scores from priors and the first emission.
  2. Add every previous-to-current transition score.
  3. Keep the best predecessor for each current state.
  4. Backtrack from the best final state.

6. Assumptions and failure modes

The Markov transition model can be too sticky, while overlapping emissions make boundaries uncertain.
