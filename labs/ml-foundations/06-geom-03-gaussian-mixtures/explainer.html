Gaussian mixture models

1. The one-sentence version

Gaussian mixture models converts a particular structural assumption into a practical prediction or representation.

2. The intuition

Think of gaussian mixture models as a compact rule for deciding which observations should influence one another; the rule helps only when that notion of relatedness matches the problem.

A five-item example

If one point has component densities [0.06,0.03] and equal weights, responsibilities normalize to [2/3,1/3], not an absolute declaration.

3. The maths in plain English

\[r_{ik}=\frac{\pi_k\mathcal N(x_i\mid\mu_k,\Sigma_k)}{\sum_j\pi_j\mathcal N(x_i\mid\mu_j,\Sigma_j)}\]

rik is row i's responsibility for component k, πk its prevalence, μk its center, and Σk its shape and orientation.

4. What the model is actually doing during training

  1. Initialize component weights, means, and covariances.
  2. E-step: compute each row's responsibility for every component.
  3. M-step: update parameters using responsibilities as fractional counts.
  4. Repeat until log likelihood improvement is negligible.

6. Assumptions and failure modes

Wrong covariance restrictions distort anisotropic groups, while too many components use Gaussians to memorize density details.

8. Where it's actually used

  • Soft customer archetypes with uncertain membership.
  • Speaker or acoustic-state density models.
  • Model-based image colour segmentation.
  • Probabilistic initialization for downstream latent models.

9. How it relates to its neighbours

k-means is a limiting equal-spherical-variance hard-assignment case. Bayesian mixtures regularize component count. DBSCAN models connected density without Gaussian shapes.
