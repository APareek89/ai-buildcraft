ARIMA and exponential smoothing

1. The one-sentence version

ARIMA and exponential smoothing converts a particular structural assumption into a practical prediction or representation.

2. The intuition

Think of arima and exponential smoothing as a compact rule for deciding which observations should influence one another; the rule helps only when that notion of relatedness matches the problem.

A five-item example

For levels [10,12,15,16,18], first differences are [2,3,1,2]. ARIMA models the more stable changes, then cumulatively adds forecasts back to the level.

3. The maths in plain English

\[\phi(B)(1-B)^dy_t=c+\theta(B)\varepsilon_t\]

B shifts one time step back, d is the differencing count, φ captures autoregression, θ moving-average shock memory, and ε an unpredictable innovation.

4. What the model is actually doing during training

  1. Difference the level to represent stable changes.
  2. Fit AR and MA dependence plus a seasonal term by likelihood.
  3. ETS recursively updates level, trend, and seasonal states.
  4. Forecast from the final state and compare at rolling historical origins.

6. Assumptions and failure modes

Ignoring trend/seasonality violates stationary-residual assumptions and produces biased forecasts or misleading intervals after shifts.

8. Where it's actually used

  • Inventory replenishment for a single established SKU.
  • Monthly financial planning with seasonality.
  • Workload forecasts for staffed services.
  • Short-horizon sensor baseline prediction.

9. How it relates to its neighbours
